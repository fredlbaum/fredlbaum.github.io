The **Icom IC-735** is my HF station, or it will be once it has something to talk through. It was a gift from **K0FMB**, and I'm looking forward to putting it on the air properly.

### The Radio

Icom released the IC-735 in 1985 as a compact, all solid-state HF transceiver, small enough for a desk corner or a vehicle. It covers all the HF amateur bands from 160 through 10 meters, including the WARC bands (30, 17, and 12 meters), and its general-coverage receiver tunes everything from 100 kHz to 30 MHz.

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Transmit** | 160–10 m, including WARC bands |
| **Receive** | 0.1–30 MHz general coverage |
| **Modes** | SSB, CW, AM, FM |
| **Power output** | About 100 W (SSB/CW/FM), 40 W AM; adjustable down to 10 W |
| **Power supply** | 13.8 V DC ±15%; about 1.2–1.5 A receive, up to 20 A transmit |
| **Size / weight** | 9.5 × 3.7 × 10.7 in, about 11 lb |
| **Made** | Japan, from 1985 |

### Power: A Marine Battery

Instead of a bench power supply, the IC-735 runs from a **marine battery kept topped up by a trickle charger**. A charged 12 V battery sits around 12.6 V, well inside the radio's 13.8 V ±15% range, and it easily covers the 20 A the rig can pull on transmit. As a bonus, the station keeps working when the grid doesn't, which is exactly when radio tends to matter most. (Whatever the source, an inline fuse on the power lead is cheap insurance.)

### The Antenna Problem

Here's the catch: right now the IC-735 has no antenna. I live in suburbia, where small lots, close neighbors, and sometimes neighborhood rules all get in the way of the big wire antennas HF really likes. The usual suburban workarounds are a wire tucked into the trees or the attic, an end-fed antenna run along the yard, or something that blends in rather than stands out. Figuring out which one fits here is the next project.

I do have the feed line sorted. I've set aside a few hundred feet of **LMR-400**, a thick, low-loss coax that's frankly overkill at HF frequencies, which means one less thing to worry about once the antenna question is answered.

### First Step: Listening

The plan is to start simple: string up a **long wire** and just listen for a while. The IC-735's general-coverage receiver makes that worthwhile on its own, from the ham bands to shortwave broadcasters, and listening is a good way to learn which bands are open, when, and from where before I start transmitting.

### What's Next

The other item on the to-do list is **upgrading my license**, which will open up more of what this radio can do. Between that and a real antenna, the IC-735 should graduate from "in the shack" to "on the air." I'll update this page as it happens.

73, and thanks again to K0FMB.
